# Chapter 4. Early-warning modelling and temporal validation

## 4.1. Modelling rationale

The empirical comparison uses logistic regression and XGBoost. Logistic
regression provides a transparent benchmark for assessing the added predictive
value of nonlinear relationships, consistent with empirical credit-scoring
comparisons [1]. XGBoost is included as a scalable gradient-boosting method
[52], because the relation between loan characteristics, payment status, and
subsequent deterioration need not be linear. Better predictive performance is
not interpreted as evidence of a causal relation between a feature and outcome.

To keep algorithm comparisons comparable, pre-specified regularised
specifications with bounded computational complexity were used. No automated
hyperparameter search — grid search, random search, Bayesian optimisation, or
Optuna — was performed. The parameters are therefore not interpreted as
optimal for Fannie Mae; they define a reproducible baseline applied uniformly
to comparable samples. Neither validation nor OOT data were used to tune them.

## 4.2. Temporal validation design

Observations are split by calendar time: January 2006–December 2016 for
training, January 2017–December 2020 for validation, and January
2021–September 2025 for the independent out-of-time test. September 2025 is
the latest admissible prediction month because the source ends in March 2026
and a complete six-month outcome window is required. The design is shown in
**Figure 4.1** — [“Chronological split”](../../../reports/figures/splits/fannie_temporal_split_timeline_v01.png).
Temporal rather than random splitting follows dynamic credit-scoring practice
[40], [39].

Transformations, feature selection, sampling, and calibration are determined
without access to the out-of-time period. The loan identifier is used for
linkage and audit only, not as a predictor. Repeated monthly records of the
same loan are therefore consistent with dynamic monitoring and do not expose
the model to identifiers or future-month values. The sample composition is
reported in **Table 4.1** — [“Temporal-split summary”](../../../reports/splits/fannie_temporal_split_v01_summary.csv).

## 4.3. Evaluation criteria

ROC-AUC and PR-AUC assess ranking performance. Calibration assesses whether
predicted probabilities agree with observed frequencies. For a binary outcome
$y_i \in \{0,1\}$, the Brier score is
$\operatorname{BS}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i-\hat{p}_i)^2$; lower values
indicate smaller squared probability errors [53]. Alert thresholds are also
evaluated by precision, recall, false-positive burden, and lead time, since
ranking alone cannot justify a review policy.

## 4.4. Data volume and actual training samples

The extended Q1+Q3 workstream contains 467,638,404 loan-month observations and
8,272,539 loans counted across separate cohort panels. The complete volume is
used to construct panels and outcomes, for descriptive analysis, and to create
deterministic samples; it is not loaded as a single training matrix.

For formal adverse status, the case-control training sample contains 1,262,399
observations, including 420,588 events. The validation and independent
out-of-time samples contain 1,034,605 and 1,748,449 observations at natural
event rates. For early deterioration, the equivalent values are 1,240,046
(396,152 events), 1,022,461, and 1,732,763. Samples are generated by a
reproducible deterministic hash rule; calibration uses validation only and
final assessment uses the out-of-time period only [54].

Relative to the pooled Q1+Q3 panel, the training samples represent about
0.270% for formal adverse status and 0.265% for early deterioration. These are
volume ratios, not simple random-sampling fractions: the denominator includes
all panel periods, whereas training is confined to an earlier interval and the
case-control procedure deliberately enriches rare events. Natural event rates
are retained for validation and out-of-time assessment.

## 4.5. Training-size sensitivity analysis

Increasing training volume does not imply retraining every candidate algorithm.
The analysis is confined to XGBoost, already selected as the leading candidate,
and is conducted separately for each outcome. Nested 1%, 5%, 10%, and 25%
natural-rate samples were formed from the Q1 training period through a
deterministic hash rule. These fractions refer to the eligible training
population rather than the pooled Q1+Q3 panel.

Models and isotonic calibrators were frozen before OOT evaluation. The eight
models were then applied once to the unchanged Q1 out-of-time sample. The
formal-adverse Red rule was pre-specified at the top 1% of scores and the
early-deterioration Amber rule at the top 5%. These are fixed expert-review
capacities, not numerical thresholds optimised on the test period.

Results are reported in **Table 4.4** — [“Training-size sensitivity: OOT
metrics”](../../../reports/train_size_sensitivity_v01/train_size_oot_metrics_summary_v01.csv)
and **Figures 4.4–4.7**: [formal-adverse metrics](../../../reports/figures/train_size_sensitivity_v01/formal_adverse_6m_train_size_oot_metrics_v01.png),
[early-deterioration metrics](../../../reports/figures/train_size_sensitivity_v01/early_deterioration_6m_train_size_oot_metrics_v01.png),
[formal-adverse reliability](../../../reports/figures/train_size_sensitivity_v01/formal_adverse_6m_train_size_oot_calibration_v01.png),
and [early-deterioration reliability](../../../reports/figures/train_size_sensitivity_v01/early_deterioration_6m_train_size_oot_calibration_v01.png).
For formal adverse, raw ROC-AUC rises from 0.878769 at 1% to 0.892613 at 25%,
but PR-AUC, Brier score, and Red-queue precision do not improve consistently.
For early deterioration, the ROC-AUC gain is 0.003456, while the 1% sample has
the lowest Brier score and the highest Amber precision and recall. The complete
interpretation and its limitations are recorded in the [experiment report](../25_train_size_sensitivity_oot_execution_report.md).

The 1% fraction is not claimed to be statistically or economically optimal. It
records the absence of a consistent operational gain in one Q1 OOT slice when
training expands to 25%; it does not show that additional history harms model
quality. The 1% natural-rate samples, containing 622,812 observations for
formal adverse and 609,823 for early deterioration, remain the resource-
efficient reproducible prototype configuration.

### 4.5.1. Comparison of prediction horizons

A separate experiment compares three-, six-, and twelve-month horizons using a
common OOT end date of March 2025. This prevents short horizons from gaining a
mechanical advantage through access to later observations. Formal-adverse
ROC-AUC values are 0.938444, 0.871818, and 0.846222; early-deterioration values
are 0.726818, 0.726690, and 0.717588. Absolute PR-AUC and precision rise with
event prevalence, so the inference also uses PR lift and precision lift; both
prevalence-adjusted measures decline as the horizon lengthens.
The rise in absolute precision at twelve months reflects the higher base event
rate in a longer future window rather than better ranking: for formal adverse,
precision lift falls from 76.385 at three months to 32.059 at twelve months;
for early deterioration it falls from 3.856 to 3.285. Results appear
in **Table 4.5** — [“Common-OOT horizon comparison”](../../../reports/horizon_sensitivity_v01/horizon_oot_comparability_summary_v01.csv)
and **Figures 4.8–4.9** — [formal adverse](../../../reports/figures/horizon_sensitivity_v01/formal_adverse_horizon_oot_comparability_v01.png)
and [early deterioration](../../../reports/figures/horizon_sensitivity_v01/early_deterioration_horizon_oot_comparability_v01.png).

## 4.6. Empirical model results

On the out-of-time sample, logistic regression obtains ROC-AUC values of
0.8794 for formal adverse status and 0.7254 for early deterioration. Isotonic
calibration reduces its Brier score from 0.021646 to 0.004320 for formal
adverse and from 0.059429 to 0.022507 for early deterioration. These results
provide a calibrated linear benchmark rather than a final model selection. They
are reported in **Tables 4.2–4.3** — [“Logistic baseline”](../../../reports/models/logistic_baseline_v01_summary.csv)
and [“Calibration summary”](../../../reports/models/logistic_isotonic_calibration_v01_summary.csv) —
and in **Figures 4.2–4.3** — [“Formal-adverse reliability”](../../../reports/figures/models/formal_adverse_6m_calibration_v01.png)
and [“Early-deterioration reliability”](../../../reports/figures/models/early_deterioration_6m_calibration_v01.png).

XGBoost obtains ROC-AUC of 0.8943 and PR-AUC of 0.2866 for formal adverse
status, and 0.7310 and 0.0663 for early deterioration, on the independent
out-of-time period. The comparison appears in [Table 6.1](../../../reports/models/final_model_comparison_v01.csv).
Within this sample, the findings support XGBoost as the leading model; Chapter
6 discusses the limits of that conclusion.
